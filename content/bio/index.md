Byol Kim is an Assistant Professor in the [Department of Statistics](https://stat.sookmyung.ac.kr/) at Sookmyung Women's University, which she joined in September 2024. Before that she was a postdoctoral scholar in the Department of Biostatistics at the University of Washington and a UW Data Science Postdoctoral Fellow at the eScience Institute, working with [Ali Shojaie](https://faculty.washington.edu/ashojaie/).

She earned her PhD in Statistics from the University of Chicago in 2021, advised by [Rina Foygel Barber](https://rinafb.github.io/) and [Mladen Kolar](https://www.marshall.usc.edu/people/mladen-kolar), with a thesis titled *Uncertainty Quantification under Weak Assumptions*. She also holds an MS in Statistics from the University of Chicago, advised by [Lek-Heng Lim](https://www.stat.uchicago.edu/~lekheng/), and a BA in Applied Mathematics from Yale, awarded *cum laude*.

Her research develops ways to quantify uncertainty while leaning on as few assumptions as possible. One line of work looks for scientifically meaningful structure in complex, high-dimensional data — such as the differential networks that capture how relationships among many variables shift from one condition to another. Another studies the black-box models now common in prediction: building predictive confidence sets with distribution-free guarantees, and asking what can, and cannot, be learned about a learning algorithm's behavior from data alone. Much of this work has grown out of collaborations with scientists in medical imaging and neuroscience.
